When is printing a better choice than embroidery?

Both branding methods have their precise market use. Neither is worse — the choice depends on the specifics of the graphic and where the logo sits on the garment. When your company’s brand book requires smooth tonal transitions, complex detail or a full gradient, ink technologically beats thread.

Printing on corporate clothing is the optimal and only sensible solution for large areas. Placing a huge embroidered logo on the back would create a stiff, uncomfortable shell that restricts an employee’s movement. Ink, by contrast, blends precisely into the fabric structure. It keeps the material’s natural flexibility and full breathability.

For large branding formats, printing techniques are also far more cost-effective to produce than large-area embroidery. At Uniforno we use two main technologies, matching them to the graphic you send.

Screen printing is a traditional, exceptionally durable method. It’s ideal for designs with a limited number of colours. It requires preparing screens, but guarantees tremendous durability and resistance to abrasion and washing at higher temperatures.

Digital printing, on the other hand, allows an almost photographic reproduction of the most complex, multi-colour designs. It removes the need to prepare separate screens for each colour, allowing complex detail to be applied flawlessly straight onto the knit fabric.
