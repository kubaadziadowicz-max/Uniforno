Embroidery vs printing —
which to choose for company t-shirts?

Investing in workwear is investing in your image. Managers who decide to unify their team’s clothing very often face a technological dilemma: embroidery vs printing. The right branding method depends on the nature of the work, the type of material and the construction of the logo itself. At Uniforno we design and make premium clothing for the B2B sector. We know the production process inside out. Let’s analyse which technique will work best for your business.

How does embroidery differ from printing?

Understanding the technology is the key to the right business decision. The fundamental difference between embroidery and printing comes down to the medium used and the final texture.

Embroidery involves physically stitching thick threads directly into the structure of the knit fabric. It requires special software and precise machines, and the final effect is three-dimensional and raised.

Printing, on the other hand, is the process of applying a special ink to the surface of the material. It delivers a completely smooth finish. This allows extremely accurate reproduction of complex designs and tonal transitions that thread simply cannot generate.

When to choose computerised embroidery?

Thread branding needs a solid, stable base. Thin t-shirts shipped in by the container from Asia quickly wrinkle and deform under the weight of dense stitching. Uniforno makes exclusively in Poland, using thick, high-weight Polish knit fabrics. Our materials stabilise any design perfectly. Choose this method if you have a minimalist logo destined for the left chest, the nape or the collar.

Advantages of embroidery

The main strength of this method is uncompromising, multi-year durability. Professional computerised embroidery on t-shirts withstands hundreds of intensive industrial wash cycles without the slightest problem. The thread doesn’t fade, doesn’t crack in the cold and doesn’t deform under the high heat of an iron. It’s an unambiguous signal of prestige, visible from afar.

Who is embroidery for?

Embroidery is a categorical requirement for industries where clothing must naturally build authority and trust. It’s the ideal solution for independent car dealerships, aesthetic-medicine clinics, prestigious hospitality and management. Raised thread looks superb on elegant polo shirts and thick sweatshirts. We fulfil company orders from just 30 pieces, supplying these sectors with top-class representative clothing.

When to choose printing?

In the embroidery vs printing contest, ink technology wins when a very large graphic has to appear on the clothing. If your brand book requires a specific gradient, shading or a multitude of small, multi-colour details — thread won’t meet the task. Durable printing on company t-shirts is the optimal choice for the back or the central chest. Modern digital techniques and screen printing deliver almost photographic precision.

Advantages of printing

Printing guarantees flexibility above all. Placing a huge embroidered logo on the back would create a stiff, heavy shell that would effectively restrict an employee’s movement. Ink blends into the material, keeping the clothing soft and breathable. It’s also the only method that allows a fully flawless reproduction of colours from a corporate Pantone palette.

Who is printing for?

It’s the most common choice for dynamic industries such as IT, modern logistics and event agencies. Software houses often have colourful, complex company mascots that need a smooth surface and perfect detail reproduction. Business-event organisers, in turn, need a large graphic on their technical staff’s backs that looks professional.
